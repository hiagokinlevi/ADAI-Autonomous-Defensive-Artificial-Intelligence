Abstract

ADAI (Autonomous Defensive Artificial Intelligence) is a proposed category of governed autonomous cyber defense. The framework specifies defended perimeters, bounded effects, declared rollback and capability-disjoint verification for systems acting on production infrastructure. Admissibility is adjudicated outside the learned model, while hash-linked, seal-terminated receipts provide a self-describing recomputation contract. The paper develops exact tamper localization, per-principal containment at shared enforcement points, element-wise reconciliation under aggregation, atomization conditions, blinded correlation and conditions for composite reversibility. It separates policy quality, envelope integrity and evidence integrity in its evaluation methodology. Formal results depend on their stated assumptions. This preprint does not assert that every theoretical construction is implemented or independently validated.

Problem and approach

How can a defensive system act on production without making model confidence the authority for action? These papers place enforceable boundaries before execution and verifiable evidence after it. The LLM is part of the system, while the decision function is pure in its declared contract inputs.

Scope and limitations

Read the manuscript’s limitations alongside its results. The reported deployment covers a single environment and a limited observation period. The DAI paper does not establish superiority over a deterministic baseline on a blind adversarial harness. Its historical §8.10 distinguishes evidence-bound targeting by construction from target provenance verifiable from the receipt. ADAI’s formal constructions depend on explicit assumptions and are not all claims of implemented capability.
